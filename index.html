Henry Park · Post-learning journey · Facilitator plan

Turn the trip into news, then next quests.

Students revisit their learning journey, combine their discoveries into a newspaper poster and choose three interest-based actions to pursue through Gem Quest. The workshop moves from recalling an experience, to explaining its meaning, to planning what to do next.

Before the workshop · Slides 1–2

Preparation and opening

10 minRoom · Welcome · Llama check-in

Materials and room setup

  • Projector, slides, a visible timer and a clear signal for attention and rotation.
  • Each student: completed mission booklet, post-learning journey worksheet and pencil.
  • Each group: one A2 sheet or mahjong paper, rough paper, thick markers, colouring materials, removable tape. A3 works with shorter text.
  • One voting sticker per student, plus spare paper for absentees or forgotten booklets.
  • Tables for 6–7, numbered groups, numbered display stations with clear walking space.
Group by venue where you can. If groups mix venues, allow a combined newspaper and label each discovery with its location. Don't expect students to describe a place they didn't visit.
Check the worksheet page order. The gem choices, My Gem Quest table (Page 3) and 3-2-1 reflection (Page 4) must be easy to find. Use section names as well as page numbers.
SLIDE 1 · WELCOME AND PURPOSE · 3 MIN

Say"Your learning journey gave you experiences and discoveries. Today, you will turn those discoveries into news worth sharing, then choose a small adventure to continue your learning."

Three tasks

Revisit your missions · create your group newspaper · choose your Gem Quest.

Expectations

Everyone contributes an idea. Listen without interrupting. Explain discoveries using something you saw, heard or did.

10min opening
SLIDE 2 · WHICH LLAMA ARE YOU? · 7 MIN

Reconnect with one moment, and hear another perspective.

"I chose llama number because . One moment that made me feel this way was ."
  1. Quiet thinking. Students pick the llama that best represents how they felt during the visit.1 MIN
  2. Partner share, about 90 seconds each, using the frame.3 MIN
  3. Two or three volunteers share different responses. Ask: "What happened that made you feel that way?"2 MIN
  4. Bridge: "We can visit the same place and notice different things. Let's find out what each person discovered."1 MIN
Every feeling is valid. Curious, tired, unsure or disappointed all count. Don't make a positive emotion the expected answer. Offer a written response or a pass on public sharing.
Listen for a feeling linked to a specific moment, not only "It was fun."

SLIDES 3–4 · MISSION BOOKLET SHARING · 15 MIN

Retrieve individual learning, then build a shared bank of ideas before deciding what goes on the poster.

Student sharing frame"The mission I chose was . I saw, heard or did . I learnt . This stood out because ."
Deepening prompts
  • "What did you notice that you had not expected?"
  • "What did a person there need to know or be able to do?"
  • "Did anything connect with a subject you learn in school?"
  1. Form groups, open booklets. Seat 6–7 together; appoint a turn-taker / timekeeper.2 MIN
  2. Choose a mission that led to a discovery, surprise or new question. Point to a note, sketch or response that explains it.2 MIN
  3. Share in a round. Up to 1 minute each. Others listen and note a useful idea; no debating mid-share.7 MIN
  4. Collect ideas on rough paper. Merge repeats, aim for 5+ distinct ideas, keep names or mission references beside each.4 MIN
IF STUDENTS GET STUCK

Start with a sketch, object, demonstration or conversation they remember. No booklet? Recall orally while a peer records. Missed the visit? Contribute questions and help organise the group's verified findings.

CHECK FOR LEARNING

Every student has contributed. The group has a usable pool of ideas, supported where possible by the mission booklets.

SLIDE 5 · THE NEWSPAPER CHALLENGE · 5 MIN

Say"You are a team of student reporters. Create a newspaper poster that helps someone who was not there understand five interesting things from your learning journey."

  1. Audience and purpose. The poster should teach the reader something about the visit.1 MIN
  2. The product. Newspaper name, headline, group members, place visited, and at least five numbered boxes — each with a heading, picture and 2–3 sentences.2 MIN
  3. Check understanding. Students name the three parts of each box. Everyone contributes; the group will share later.2 MIN
Five strong boxes first. Every headline promises five items; extras become a small bonus fact. Number 1–5, or 5–1 for a countdown headline.

Choose headlines, read like reporters

15 minAllocation · Example posters

Slide 6 · 8 min. Pick one allocation method before the lesson. The quick method is easier to manage; the auction adds a playful link to the mission booklets.

OPTION A · FIRST COME, FIRST SERVED

Give 1 minute to agree on a first and second choice. One seated spokesperson signals readiness. Record choices in the order signalled; once claimed, a headline is gone. Resolve simultaneous signals with a quick draw.

RULE

No racing across the room.

Use A when group sizes or mission opportunities differ a lot.

OPTION B · MISSION POINTS AUCTION

Groups total completed missions across members' booklets: one completed mission = one point, counted per student. Accept meaningful written, drawn or supported responses. Check totals before bidding.

AUCTION RULES
  • One bidder per group, with a backup choice ready.
  • Titles in slide order, max 30 seconds each. Start at 1, raise by whole points.
  • No bidding beyond your total. Highest bid wins; deduct it and that group sits out.
  • Ties that can't be raised → draw lots. Leftover titles go to groups without one, including zero-point groups.
  • More than nine groups? Allow duplicates from the start.
Points are game currency, not a learning grade. Every group must leave with a title.

SLIDES 7–8 · READ THE EXAMPLES LIKE REPORTERS · 7 MIN
  1. Notice. Show both examples. Identify the headline, numbered sections, pictures and details.2 MIN
  2. Compare. "Which detail helps you understand what the group learnt? What makes a heading easy to scan? How does the picture explain the idea?"2 MIN
  3. Transfer. Model turning one real class observation into a heading and short explanation. Students pick one layout feature to use.3 MIN
ACCURACY REMINDER

The examples combine several venues. Students use only experiences from their own visit.

"New skills" must describe something students actually tried or can now do. Watching a professional isn't the same as mastering the skill.

Top: newspaper name, main headline, group members, place visited. Below: five numbered boxes, written large enough for a reader standing at the display.

HHeading

A short, clear name for the idea, specific to the discovery.

PPicture

An object, person, process or scene that explains the idea. A labelled sketch is enough; use a photo only if one already exists.

DDetails

2–3 sentences: "We learnt that…", "We saw or heard…", "We chose this because…". What happened and why it matters.

"It was interesting" is too general. Ask: What was interesting? What did you see or hear? What did it help you understand? Use students' own evidence, never an invented venue fact.
PLAN AND SHARE RESPONSIBILITIES · 5 MIN
  1. Select five ideas that match the headline. Remove repetition, agree the order.
  2. Sketch the layout lightly before any colour.
  3. Assign a lead per box. Pairs can share; others check headline, readability, consistency.
Editor · meaning + facts Timekeeper · pace Box leads · organise ideas Illustrator · pictures Presenters · rehearse

Roles can combine. They support participation; no one student carries all the writing or artwork.

MAKE THE POSTER · 17 MIN

Circulate. Check the five ideas and supporting details before helping with presentation. At halfway, all five boxes should be started. At the five-minute warning: finish content, then decorate.

Facilitation questions
  • "Where did you learn this?"
  • "How is this different from your other box?"
  • "What would a reader who was not there need to know?"
  • "Whose idea have we not heard yet?"

Extension: add a "Now we wonder…" question or an "Our big takeaway" sentence once all five boxes are done.

CHECK AND REHEARSE · 5 MIN

Tick through the poster check with each group.

Support: pre-drawn boxes, sentence starters, a word bank or a peer scribe. Allow oral rehearsal before writing. If time is tight, accept labelled sketches and concise sentences.

Present and learn from other groups

20 minGallery rotation · Vote · Debrief
Ready0/4Presenter A hosts
RECOMMENDED · GALLERY PRESENTATIONS
  1. Setup and briefing. Posters at numbered stations. Two presenters per group: A hosts rounds 1–2, B hosts 3–4. Practise the attention signal.3 MIN
  2. Four rounds of 2½ min: 90s presenting, 30s visitor question, 30s to move. Visitors go to the next station, skipping their own.10 MIN
  3. Presenter swap after round 2. B returns to host; A joins the group at its next destination.1 MIN
  4. Vote and debrief. Final look, one sticker each, then two or three voices.6 MIN

With six groups, each travelling group visits four other posters. Fewer than five groups: one visit each, spare time to discussion. Number routes in advance so two groups never arrive together. Seated sharing can replace walking.

The 90-second presentation
0–15 SIntroduce the group, venue and headline.
15–65 SBriefly name all five discoveries. Spend more time on the most significant one and explain the evidence.
65–90 SShare the group's biggest takeaway or one question the visit raised.
VISITOR TASK

Listen for one discovery that's new to you, or a different perspective. Ask: "What made that surprising?" or "How did you find that out?"

VOTE · 2 + 2 + 2 MIN

One sticker each for the poster that best helped you understand the journey. Not your own group. Look for clear learning, specific detail and pictures that explain.

USING THE VOTE

Stickers are audience feedback, not an assessment score. Don't judge artistic ability or treat a low count as a verdict on the group.

ALTERNATIVE · WHOLE-CLASS PRESENTATIONS · SAME 20 MIN

2 min setup · 12 min for six groups (90s talk + 30s question) · 6 min review, vote and debrief. At least two speakers per group. More than six groups? Shorten talks or add time — never cut the individual reflection.

Choose and plan the Gem Quest

15 minIntro 3 · Select 4 · Record 5 · Buddy 3

Say"Your learning journey may be over, but your discoveries can continue. A side quest is a small action you choose because something made you curious. Pick three gems that help you explore an interest. You do not need to choose a future job today."

The gem menu on slide 11

Try it · pick three

If the printed worksheet has further gems, explain those too. Students can pursue one interest through three actions, or explore more than one. Use the worksheet's own labels when recording.

SELECT THREE GEMS · 4 MIN
"I became interested in when ."

Students mark three actions and explain how each connects to an interest. Check feasibility: manageable time, materials and support. Prefer free school, library or home options.

RECORD A PLAN IN MY GEM QUEST · 5 MIN

In the Page 3 table, for each gem: the specific action, an intended date, and a person or resource who can help.

Plan ≠ done. The column reads "What I did / discovered". Students write PLAN: I will… in pencil now, then replace it with what they actually did. Only then do they tick and colour the gem. Emerald needs practice on three different days.
Illustrative plan"Sapphire — PLAN: By Friday, I will find out how a robot detects an obstacle, using a school library resource with my teacher's help. Afterwards, I will write one thing I discovered."
BUDDY CHECK · 3 MIN

Students share one planned action. The partner asks three questions:

What exactly?

When?

Who or what helps?

Reflect and carry it forward

10 min3-2-1 checkout · Partner commitment

Page 4 reflection. Introduce 1 min · write 5 min · review 2 min. Answers show their own learning, even after a group poster. Brief phrases, supported writing or an adult-recorded oral response are fine.

"Your answers should show your own learning, even though you worked on a group poster."

PARTNER COMMITMENT · 2 MIN
"One interest I want to explore is . My first step is . I will do it by ."

Invite two brief commitments, then close.

FOLLOW-UP

Agree a check-in with the teacher, ideally within one to two weeks. Keep worksheets accessible. At the check-in, students share an action, evidence of what they did, and a discovery or changed question.

Gem completion is based on action taken, not on choosing a gem in class.
